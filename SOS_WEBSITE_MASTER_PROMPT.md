# Master Build Prompt: Students Organize for Syria Website Rebrand

## Role and Objective

Act as a senior nonprofit brand strategist, UX designer, copywriter, motion designer, accessibility specialist, and WordPress developer. Completely redesign and rebuild the public website for **Students Organize for Syria (SOS)** using the current website as a content source:

- Current website: <https://organize4syria.com/>
- Visual moodboard: <https://www.pinterest.com/razaankii/sos-rebrand/?invite_code=09a17b734fd6404894e89c59b7bdc2c3&sender=486951915868004742>
- Movement-design references:
  - <https://marchforourlives.org/>
  - <https://www.sunrisemovement.org/>
- Long-term chapter-network reference:
  - <https://www.netimpact.org/>

The result must feel like a living, student-led movement—not a conventional nonprofit template and not a leadership institute. The website should communicate youth ownership, community power, cultural pride, activist credibility, and a confident vision for Syria’s future.

Do not reproduce the layouts, copy, graphics, or proprietary design elements of any reference site. Study their information hierarchy, movement energy, campaign visibility, proof-of-impact structure, and chapter-network organization, then create an original SOS design.

---

## Core Strategic Shift

Reposition SOS from:

> Students responding to a crisis

to:

> **Students shaping the future of Syria.**

The central brand idea is:

> **Building the next generation of Syrian advocates, leaders, and changemakers.**

SOS is first and foremost a **student movement for Syria**. Leadership development, partnerships, training, mentorship, conferences, and professional pathways support the movement; they do not replace or define it.

The website must communicate:

- Students are already leading—not merely preparing to lead someday.
- SOS organizes young people and builds community power.
- Chapters turn campuses into places of advocacy, connection, fundraising, education, and action.
- Syria is not presented only through tragedy or crisis.
- Young Syrians and allies are active participants in shaping the future.
- SOS combines grassroots energy with institutional credibility.
- The movement is bold, unapologetic, hopeful, ambitious, and polished.

## Audience

Design for these primary audiences:

1. Students who want to join or start an SOS chapter.
2. Existing chapter leaders and members.
3. Young Syrian advocates and diaspora communities.
4. University administrators and campus partners.
5. Nonprofit, advocacy, humanitarian, and professional partners.
6. Donors and supporters.
7. Journalists and people seeking official SOS statements.

The homepage must allow a first-time visitor to understand within seconds:

- What SOS is.
- What SOS is building.
- Why the work matters now.
- What students actually do.
- How to join, start a chapter, support a campaign, or donate.

## Brand Voice

Use concise, active, movement-centered language.

The voice should be:

- Bold, direct, and unapologetic.
- Youthful without sounding childish.
- Hopeful without becoming vague or sentimental.
- Politically and culturally aware.
- Credible enough for partners and donors.
- Human rather than corporate.
- Focused on collective power rather than individual achievement.

Prefer language such as:

- We organize.
- We build.
- We speak up.
- We shape what comes next.
- Students are not waiting for the future.
- From campus to community.
- Built by students. Powered by community.
- Syria’s future belongs to those willing to build it.

Avoid:

- Generic nonprofit phrases such as “making a difference.”
- Savior language.
- Trauma-centered imagery and messaging.
- Presenting Syrians as passive recipients.
- Excessive corporate terminology.
- Positioning SOS primarily as a fellowship, career program, or leadership academy.
- Invented achievements, statistics, chapter counts, dates, quotations, or active campaigns.

## Visual Direction

Create a modern editorial identity that combines grassroots organizing with professional execution.

### Typography

- Use oversized, tightly composed headlines.
- Use a bold sans-serif system with an editorial feeling.
- Preferred directions: Neue Haas Grotesk, Inter, Söhne, or General Sans.
- If a commercial font is unavailable, use **Inter** or a high-quality open-source alternative with similar proportions.
- Use strong contrast between huge display headlines, compact labels, and highly readable body text.
- Headlines may break across lines intentionally and occasionally use outlined, condensed, italic, or offset words for emphasis.
- Avoid generic centered nonprofit headings throughout the page.

### Color System

Reflect the Syrian flag subtly and sophisticatedly. Do not make the site look like a literal flag graphic.

Primary colors:

- Deep charcoal: approximately `#171816`
- Off-white: approximately `#F4F1E8`

Accent colors:

- Strong movement green inspired by the flag: approximately `#148447`
- Warm cream: approximately `#E8DDC5`
- Muted red: approximately `#A8483D`

Support colors may include a restrained ink black and a slightly faded archival gray. Meet WCAG AA color-contrast requirements.

### Photography

The people are the brand. Prioritize documentary-style photographs of:

- Student organizers.
- Chapter meetings.
- Conferences and leadership summits.
- Workshops and training sessions.
- Speakers and panel discussions.
- Campus actions and advocacy.
- Networking events.
- Students planning, speaking, listening, collaborating, and celebrating.

Do not lead with generic photographs of Syria, ruins, war, refugee camps, flags, or passive humanitarian subjects. Syria may appear through culture, community, language, pattern, memory, and student identity rather than crisis imagery.

Images should carry movement and atmosphere through:

- Intentional motion blur.
- Slight vibration or chromatic offset.
- Saturated green or muted-red overlays.
- Grain, halftone, risograph, photocopy, paper, or archival-print texture.
- Bold crops and off-center framing.
- Layered captions, tape shapes, borders, and editorial annotations.

Use the supplied Pinterest board as a **moodboard and visual-reference source**. Use any downloaded Pinterest image directly on the public site only if SOS owns it or has confirmed publication rights. Otherwise, recreate the visual principles using SOS-owned photography and original textures. Preserve photographer credits and alt text.

## Motion and Interaction Direction

Motion should make the website feel alive and organized, not gimmicky.

Include:

- A refined loading or page-entry sequence using the SOS wordmark or headline.
- Staggered hero typography reveals.
- Scroll-triggered text and image reveals.
- Masked image entrances and subtle parallax.
- A continuous but calm campaign or movement ticker.
- Hover states with color inversions, underline movement, or image shifts.
- Small image vibration or chromatic separation on hover.
- Animated statistics only when figures have been verified.
- Campaign cards that feel physical and poster-like.
- Smooth anchor navigation and page transitions where technically appropriate.

Motion requirements:

- Honor `prefers-reduced-motion` and provide a calm static experience.
- Avoid scroll hijacking.
- Avoid excessive floating elements or constant motion.
- Keep animations performant using transforms and opacity where possible.
- Make all interactions work with keyboard, touch, and screen readers.

## Homepage Structure

### 1. Utility Announcement Bar

Use for one current priority: an active campaign, chapter application deadline, conference, or urgent statement. It must be editable in WordPress without code.

### 2. Header and Navigation

Include:

- SOS logo/wordmark.
- About.
- Our Work.
- Campaigns.
- Chapters.
- News & Statements.
- Get Involved.
- A highly visible Donate or Take Action button.
- Mobile navigation with strong motion and full keyboard support.

### 3. Hero

Use a bold first-viewport statement such as:

> **Students are shaping the future of Syria.**

Supporting copy should explain that SOS is a student-led movement organizing campuses and communities to advocate, build power, and create what comes next.

Primary actions:

- Join the movement.
- Start a chapter.

Secondary action:

- Explore our work.

Use one powerful SOS-owned student photograph or a fast, restrained sequence of two or three images. The first viewport must feel unmistakably human, energetic, and student-led.

### 4. Movement Statement

Explain the strategic shift in a strong editorial layout:

> SOS began with students responding to a crisis. Today, students are organizing to shape Syria’s future.

Clarify that chapters, advocacy, community building, campaigns, and leadership support form one movement.

### 5. Accomplishments and Proof

Place verified accomplishments near the top of the homepage, inspired by the clarity of March For Our Lives.

Possible content from the existing site that may be retained only after verification:

- Collaboration with Karam Foundation on educational support.
- Advocacy in Washington, D.C. with the Syrian American Medical Society.
- English tutoring with Paper Airplanes.
- Winter-coat fundraising with NuDay Syria Foundation.
- Partnerships with the Syrian American Council and Syrian Community Network.
- National conferences in Chicago and Ann Arbor.
- The 2017 SAMS Organization of the Year recognition.

Do not publish placeholder numbers or outdated crisis statistics. If verified metrics are unavailable, use specific accomplishment statements rather than fabricated counters.

### 6. Active Campaigns

Place current campaigns front and center, inspired by Sunrise Movement’s campaign hierarchy.

Each campaign card should contain:

- Campaign name.
- One-sentence objective.
- Status: active, upcoming, or completed.
- Strong image or graphic treatment.
- Primary action.
- Optional progress or verified result.

If no current campaigns are provided, create the section structure with clearly labeled CMS placeholders but do not invent campaign names or publish fake content.

### 7. What the Movement Does

Organize the work into a small number of clear pillars:

1. **Organize campuses** — build student chapters and local community power.
2. **Advocate for Syria** — center informed Syrian voices and move public conversation.
3. **Mobilize resources** — fundraise and activate support around concrete needs.
4. **Develop people** — provide training, mentorship, partnerships, conferences, and professional pathways that help organizers sustain the movement.

The fourth pillar supports the first three and must not dominate the narrative.

### 8. Interactive College and Chapter Network

Create a prominent, highly visual section inspired by Net Impact’s moving chapter network, but use an original execution.

Desktop experience:

- Display all active SOS colleges/chapters in a continuously rotating orbital network, horizontal marquee, or circular constellation.
- College names or logos move at a calm, readable speed.
- Pause movement when a visitor hovers, focuses, or presses a pause control.
- Each college is a real accessible link.
- Clicking or activating a college opens its individual SOS chapter page.
- The active/focused college becomes visually prominent and may reveal its city, state, chapter status, year established, and featured campaign.

Mobile experience:

- Replace the orbit with a swipeable, accessible chapter rail or stacked card list.
- Do not force tiny moving targets on mobile.

Accessibility and usability:

- Include a visible “Pause animation” control.
- Support keyboard arrow navigation where appropriate.
- Provide a searchable/filterable chapter directory below or alongside the animation.
- The information must remain available when animation is disabled.
- Do not fabricate colleges. Populate the animation from a WordPress Chapter content type and display only verified active chapters.

Every individual chapter page should support:

- College or university name.
- City and state.
- Chapter status.
- Chapter photograph.
- Short chapter story.
- Programs or campaigns.
- Social links.
- Public contact method.
- Year established.
- Join-this-chapter action.

### 9. Student Voices

Use real quotes, short stories, or chapter spotlights from students. Prioritize photographs and lived experience. Do not invent testimonials. If none are supplied, create CMS-ready placeholders that remain unpublished.

### 10. News and Statements

Show recent movement news, official statements, chapter stories, and campaign updates. Each card needs content type, date, headline, excerpt, and image.

### 11. Final Action Section

End with a direct movement invitation rather than a generic newsletter block:

> **Do not watch the future happen. Help shape it.**

Offer clear paths:

- Join a chapter.
- Start a chapter.
- Partner with SOS.
- Donate.
- Subscribe for updates.

### 12. Footer

Include navigation, social links, contact information, nonprofit/legal details, privacy policy, accessibility statement, and external portal/resource links where applicable.

## Content Migration and Accuracy Rules

Use <https://organize4syria.com/> as a historical content source, not as the design model.

Preserve useful verified history, including the chapter model, past campaigns, partnerships, conference history, and participation routes. Audit every claim before publication.

Specifically:

- Do not reuse the current site’s incorrect “120 million Syrians” displacement figure.
- Do not reuse outdated humanitarian statistics without a current authoritative source and date.
- Do not imply an old campaign is currently active.
- Do not fabricate chapter names, statistics, member counts, testimonials, quotes, dates, awards, or partnerships.
- Mark content requiring SOS verification in the CMS as draft.
- Preserve important existing URLs with redirects when URLs change.
- Retain search value and historical archives where the content remains accurate.

## WordPress Architecture

The production website must remain on WordPress and be built on a private staging environment before launch.

Use a stable, maintainable foundation with:

- Native Gutenberg blocks and patterns or another carefully justified stable editor system.
- A lightweight custom theme or child theme.
- Reusable global styles, patterns, sections, buttons, cards, and templates.
- Advanced Custom Fields only where structured fields genuinely improve editing.
- Minimal, reputable plugins.
- No dependence on one developer for routine content updates.
- No hardcoded chapter, campaign, statistics, or announcement content.

Create custom content types for:

- Chapters.
- Campaigns.
- News and statements.
- Events/conferences.
- Partners, if needed.

Create taxonomies or structured fields for:

- Region, city, state, and chapter status.
- Campaign status and campaign type.
- News type.
- Event date and location.

Editor roles should be simplified so communications staff and chapter administrators see only the tools they need. Routine editors must be able to:

- Update homepage announcements.
- Add or edit chapters.
- Add campaigns and campaign results.
- Publish news and statements.
- Upload events.
- Update leadership or contact information.
- Manage external links.

They should not need to manage code, plugins, hosting, security, or theme settings.

## Accessibility, Performance, and Quality

Meet WCAG 2.2 AA standards wherever reasonably possible.

Requirements:

- Semantic heading hierarchy.
- Visible focus indicators.
- Keyboard-operable menus, controls, filters, carousels, and animations.
- Pause controls for moving content.
- Reduced-motion support.
- Descriptive alt text.
- Captions or transcripts for audiovisual content.
- Accessible form labels, errors, and success messages.
- Sufficient color contrast.
- Minimum touch-target sizes.
- No essential information communicated through color alone.

Performance requirements:

- Optimize all images and use responsive sizes.
- Lazy-load below-the-fold media.
- Avoid autoplay video with sound.
- Keep animation libraries lightweight.
- Minimize plugins, scripts, layout shifts, and render-blocking assets.
- Target strong Core Web Vitals on representative mobile connections.

Quality assurance must cover:

- Current versions of major browsers.
- Common mobile and tablet sizes.
- Keyboard-only navigation.
- Screen-reader landmarks and labels.
- Reduced-motion mode.
- Form validation.
- Broken links and redirect mapping.
- Chapter directory search and filters.
- Every college link in the animated network.

## Definition of Success

The redesign succeeds when:

- SOS is immediately understood as a student movement shaping Syria’s future.
- Students can find or start a chapter without confusion.
- Active campaigns and verified accomplishments are visible early.
- The college network feels alive while remaining usable and accessible.
- The people of SOS—not stock imagery or crisis imagery—carry the brand.
- Leadership development appears as movement infrastructure, not the organization’s primary identity.
- The design feels bold, editorial, culturally thoughtful, activist, and polished.
- Routine staff can update the site without technical expertise.
- The WordPress foundation can expand into a larger chapter and member platform later.

Before implementation, present the sitemap, content gaps, design tokens, animation plan, WordPress content model, and page-by-page build plan. Then create the complete website, validate it, and prepare it for review on a private staging site before any public launch.
